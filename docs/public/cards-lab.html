Three pairings for the two cards below the hero

Each row shows the "get started" card and the "sample gallery" card at the real homepage size, with working hover states. Hover a card, or freeze the hover state in the toolbar, to see the motion. Every document shown is a real render of the library (the tutorial invoice and pages of the Component Variants gallery document); yellow ribbons mark where a different real render is still needed.

Recommendation: row A. A framed video poster next to a contact sheet of pages. The two cards are unmistakably different things, each is clickable at a glance, and together they need one poster frame plus a folder of gallery renders to keep correct. Details and the reasoning are at the foot of the page.

A

Player frame + contact sheet

Why it gets readA play button in a 16:9 frame is the one shape every visitor recognises as "video", and a grid of eight page thumbnails reads as "a gallery" before any word is read. One big image against many small ones keeps the row from looking like two versions of the same card.
MotionLeft: on hover the poster drifts down through the invoice (7s), the play button grows and turns blue, and the progress bar starts filling under chapter ticks. Right: on hover every page thumbnail slowly pans from its header to its body (6s, staggered per column); the tile under the pointer lifts. Entering the viewport only completes a short rise.
UpkeepLowest of the three. The poster is one crop of the tutorial invoice, re-rendered when the invoice changes. The grid takes eight pages straight from the gallery renders; a build step can pick them, so the card follows the gallery. Duration and chapter positions are two numbers.

Critique

Gimmicks

  • G5 loupe and V5 filmstrip: metaphors that entertain for a second and explain nothing about what is behind the click.
  • V1's progress bar is on the edge: a filling bar that plays nothing is a small lie. Keep the ticks, keep the fill short, or drop the fill.
  • G4 animates a UI that does not exist. If the gallery page ends up without filters, the card is a promise the page breaks.

Will look dated in a year

  • G3 perspective shelf: the tilted wall of screenshots is already the most recognisable template look.
  • V5 filmstrip: sprocket holes.
  • V1 survives only if the chrome stays minimal; glassy player skins date, a plain play circle does not.
  • V4 and G1 are the least fashion-bound: a document and a grid of documents.

Pairings

  • A (V1 + G1): strongest contrast in the row, one framed image against many small pages. Both are honest about what they link to.
  • B (V2 + G2): the richest story, but two text-heavy visuals and a second code window under the quick start; the row gets busy.
  • C (V4 + G4): both are clever and both are interactive; together they ask for two hovers to make sense, and C1 has no video cue at rest.
  • Avoid pairing V3 with G2 (two code windows), or V5 with G3 (two sliding strips).

Recommendation

Row A. It fixes every listed problem directly: the whole card is a link with a lift and an arrow, the play button makes the left card a video at a glance, the grid makes the right card a gallery at a glance, the copy is one sentence each, and the hover motion is slow and local. It also stays correct with the least effort: the poster is a crop of the tutorial invoice and the grid can be generated from the gallery's render folder. One borrow from row B is worth keeping: the chapter ticks on the progress bar, which cost nothing and tell the skeptical developer that the video is structured.
